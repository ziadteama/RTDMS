# 2.1 Literature Survey

↑ [[02 Literature Survey]]

Bhatt and Joshi's 2025 survey, *State-of-the-Art in Driver's Drowsiness Detection: A Comprehensive Survey*, provides a detailed overview of modern driver fatigue detection technologies by reviewing more than one hundred research studies. The authors classify drowsiness detection methods into three main categories: behavioural, physiological, and vehicle-based approaches. Behavioural methods mainly depend on computer vision techniques to analyse visible facial indicators such as eye closure, blinking patterns, yawning, and head movements. The survey also discusses physiological monitoring methods, including EEG, ECG, and heart-rate variability, which measure biological changes associated with fatigue.

Fu et al.'s 2024 survey, *A Survey on Drowsiness Detection: Modern Applications and Methods*, examines recent fatigue detection technologies used in transportation and other safety-critical environments. The paper reviews both single-sensor and multimodal monitoring systems, including camera-based behavioural analysis, physiological signal monitoring, and machine learning classification methods. It highlights how modern systems can detect driver fatigue by analysing visual cues such as facial features, eye movement, and head orientation, while also using physiological signals to improve detection accuracy.

Hassan et al.'s 2024 study, *Deep Learning-Based Driver Drowsiness Detection Using Facial Expression Analysis*, presents a real-time system for detecting driver fatigue using convolutional neural networks and computer vision. The system analyses facial expressions captured by an in-vehicle camera to identify common signs of drowsiness, such as eye closure, yawning, and head tilt. It uses OpenCV to detect and extract facial features, then applies a CNN model to classify the driver's state as alert or drowsy.

Florez et al.'s 2023 study, *A CNN-Based Approach for Driver Drowsiness Detection by Real-Time Eye State Identification*, focuses on detecting driver drowsiness through computer vision and deep learning. The authors propose a real-time system that analyses the driver's eye region using convolutional neural networks to determine whether the driver is alert or drowsy. The study evaluates three CNN architectures — VGG16, InceptionV3, and ResNet50V2 — using the NITYMED driver drowsiness dataset. Among the tested models, ResNet50V2 achieved the best performance with an average accuracy of approximately 99.7%.

Li et al.'s 2023 study, *A CNN-Based Wearable System for Driver Drowsiness Detection*, presents a wearable system designed to detect driver fatigue by analysing eye closure patterns. The system combines a lightweight convolutional neural network with a wearable device that includes a small camera placed in eyeglasses. This camera captures images of the driver's eye region, which are then processed to measure important drowsiness indicators such as blink rate and PERCLOS.

Vamshi et al.'s 2023 study, *Detection of Distracted Driver Using Convolutional Neural Networks*, investigates the use of deep convolutional neural networks for detecting distracted driving behaviour. The proposed system analyses video frames captured from an in-vehicle camera and classifies different driver activities, including mobile phone usage, drinking, reaching behind the seat, and adjusting hair or makeup. The results show that CNN-based behaviour recognition can effectively identify multiple types of distracted driving actions.

Pandey and Muppalaneni's 2022 survey, *Survey on Visual and Non-Visual Features in Driver's Drowsiness Detection*, compares the different features used to detect driver fatigue. The study explains that visual methods depend on computer vision to monitor driver behaviour through facial features such as the eyes, mouth, and head position, while non-visual methods rely on physiological signals and vehicle dynamics. The survey also highlights that combining visual and non-visual features can improve the reliability of driver fatigue detection systems.

Cui et al.'s 2021 study, *Driver Drowsiness Detection from EEG Signals Using a Convolutional Neural Network*, focuses on detecting driver fatigue through physiological monitoring using electroencephalography signals. The researchers developed a compact convolutional neural network to analyse brain activity patterns that are associated with drowsiness. The results demonstrate that EEG-based monitoring can effectively detect drowsiness by identifying neurological patterns such as alpha and theta brainwave activity.

Salman et al.'s 2021 study, *Driver Drowsiness Detection Using Ensemble Convolutional Neural Networks*, investigates driver fatigue detection using ensemble CNN models applied to driver facial images. The proposed system analyses visual indicators such as eye closure, yawning frequency, and head movement to determine the driver's alertness level. The experimental results show that the ensemble CNN model achieves an F1-score of approximately 0.935.

Mofid et al.'s 2020 study, *Distracted Driver Detection Using Convolutional Neural Networks*, focuses on identifying distracted driving behaviours using deep learning techniques. The authors developed a convolutional neural network model trained on the State Farm Distracted Driver dataset to classify different driver activities, such as texting, talking on the phone, adjusting the radio, and drinking. The study demonstrates that computer vision and deep learning techniques can effectively identify driver distractions such as smartphone use, which is a major cause of traffic accidents.

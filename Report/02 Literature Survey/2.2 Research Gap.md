# 2.2 Research Gap

↑ [[02 Literature Survey]]

Based on the analysis of the reviewed literature and the identified research gaps, the proposed driver monitoring system will focus on combining vision-based monitoring with non-intrusive physiological sensing to improve reliability. To address the problem of lighting sensitivity, the system will use robust facial feature detection techniques and optimized computer vision algorithms that can operate under different lighting conditions. To reduce errors caused by face occlusion, the system will monitor multiple behavioural indicators such as eye closure, gaze direction, and head movement rather than relying on a single facial feature.

## 2.2.1 Best Approaches for Each Feature

The best approach for vision-based drowsiness detection is eye monitoring using computer vision techniques. This method focuses on analysing eye closure, blink rate, and facial landmarks to identify early signs of fatigue. Since drowsiness often appears through visible facial indicators, especially prolonged eye closure and reduced blinking control, computer vision provides a practical and non-contact solution for real-time monitoring. Deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks, are widely recommended for this feature because they can automatically extract important visual patterns from driver images and achieve high detection accuracy.

For gaze tracking and head pose estimation, the most suitable approach is to combine eye tracking with head orientation analysis. Gaze tracking determines where the driver is looking, while head pose estimation identifies whether the driver's head is turned away from the road. By combining both techniques, the system can detect visual distraction more accurately. Driver distraction detection can also be improved using deep learning models trained on datasets such as the State Farm Distracted Driver Dataset. In addition, physiological monitoring using heart rate and heart rate variability adds an important internal layer to the system, as these signals reflect cardiovascular changes that may occur when a driver becomes tired.

The features of driver monitoring systems in the literature are reviewed for comparison purposes to identify the most suitable components for the proposed system. The main features that will be implemented include drowsiness detection using eye closure, blink rate, and PERCLOS. Gaze tracking will also be used to monitor the driver's attention. In addition, phone-use detection will be included to identify distracted driving behaviours caused by mobile phone usage. The system will also incorporate physiological monitoring using heart rate and heart rate variability through the MAX30102 sensor.

## 2.2.2 Most Appropriate Approaches for Our System

The most appropriate approach for detecting driver fatigue in the proposed system is vision-based eye monitoring using computer vision techniques. This approach analyses eye closure, blink rate, and facial landmarks to detect early signs of drowsiness while the driver is operating the vehicle. One of the most important metrics used for this purpose is PERCLOS, which stands for Percentage of Eye Closure. PERCLOS is widely used in driver fatigue detection research because it provides a reliable indication of how long the driver's eyes remain closed over a specific period.

Another important approach for the system is the combination of gaze tracking and head pose estimation. Gaze tracking helps determine where the driver is looking, while head pose estimation identifies whether the driver's head is facing the road or turned away. Combining these two techniques allows the system to detect visual distraction more accurately. The system will also include driver distraction and phone-use detection using deep learning-based image classification models.

In addition to behavioural monitoring, the proposed system will incorporate physiological monitoring to improve detection reliability. Heart rate and heart rate variability will be measured using a PPG sensor, such as the MAX30102, connected to a microcontroller and a Raspberry Pi. By combining vision-based detection, gaze and head pose analysis, phone-use detection, and HR/HRV monitoring, the system can provide a more accurate and reliable real-time driver monitoring solution.

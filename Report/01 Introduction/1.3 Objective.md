# 1.3 Objective

↑ [[01 Introduction]]

First, the system aims to monitor eye closure and calculate the PERCLOS metric using a Raspberry Pi. The goal is to process video input in real time with less than one second latency and accurately detect frequent eye closure that indicates drowsiness. Success will be measured by achieving at least 85–90% detection accuracy, reliably triggering an alert when the eyes remain closed beyond a defined threshold (such as 2–3 seconds), and maintaining stable performance under typical in-vehicle lighting conditions.

In addition, the system seeks to track head pose and gaze direction continuously to ensure the driver's attention remains focused on the road. The objective is to detect off-road gaze behaviour and activate an alert if the driver looks away for longer than a safe duration. This component will be considered successful if it achieves at least 85–90% accuracy in detecting gaze deviation, triggers the buzzer when the deviation exceeds the predefined time limit, and operates smoothly without noticeable processing delays.

The project also aims to detect mobile phone usage as a form of manual distraction. Using AI-based image classification, the system must identify when the driver is holding or interacting with a phone and respond promptly. Success will be defined by achieving at least 85–90% classification accuracy and activating the alert within one second of confirmed phone detection.

Beyond behavioural monitoring, the system incorporates physiological sensing through heart rate measurement. Heart rate signals are acquired using contact sensors, which perform real-time sampling and preprocessing before transmitting validated BPM values to the Raspberry Pi. Success will be measured by stable signal acquisition, consistent BPM estimation, and reliable detection of sustained heart rate trends. The system aims to provide an immediate audible buzzer alert whenever any monitored parameter exceeds predefined safety thresholds.

## Stakeholders

Key stakeholders play an important role in shaping the requirements, functionality, and adoption of the Real-Time Driver Monitoring System. The main end-users are private car drivers and bus drivers, who directly benefit from the system through real-time alerts such as wearable vibration or buzzer warnings. Fleet operators are also key stakeholders because they manage multiple vehicles and drivers, and they can use the system logs to support performance reviews, safety training, and insurance compliance. Another important stakeholder is the Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM), which may integrate the system into vehicles or smart dash cameras to improve safety ratings.

The user stories highlight the needs of these stakeholders. Drivers need immediate warnings through vibration or sound alerts when signs of drowsiness are detected. They also need the system to detect gaze deviation and confirm risk by checking heart rate and heart rate variability, so alerts are activated only when there is a stronger indication of danger. Fleet operators need access to phone usage logs combined with wearable sensor data to review driver behaviour patterns. Meanwhile, original equipment manufacturers require a scalable integration between the Raspberry Pi and wearable sensors so the system can be adopted as an effective safety enhancement in modern vehicles.

# 1.2 Problem Statement

↑ [[01 Introduction]]

Road accidents caused by driver fatigue, distraction, and reduced attention remain a serious safety issue worldwide. Drivers frequently experience drowsiness, gaze deviation, or engage in risky behaviours such as mobile phone use while driving. These impairments often go unnoticed until it is too late, leading to preventable accidents, injuries, and fatalities. The problem primarily affects everyday drivers in private vehicles and commercial transport environments where long driving hours and cognitive overload increase fatigue risk.

Road traffic accidents are a major global safety concern, with the World Health Organization reporting around 1.3 million deaths annually. Research shows that driver fatigue contributes to a significant percentage of crashes, and distracted driving — especially mobile phone use — greatly increases reaction time and accident risk. Studies also confirm that measurable behavioural signs (such as eye closure and gaze deviation) and physiological changes (like reduced heart rate patterns during fatigue) can indicate driver impairment before a crash occurs. These findings clearly demonstrate that driver drowsiness and distraction are real, documented problems that require effective real-time monitoring solutions.

This issue is important because delayed detection of drowsiness or distraction directly increases accident risk, threatening human life and causing economic losses. Early detection and real-time intervention can significantly reduce these risks by alerting the driver before a critical situation occurs.

The scope of this project is to address this gap by focusing on real-time detection of driver impairment through behavioural indicators (eye closure, gaze direction, phone usage) and physiological indicators (heart rate). The system is designed for in-vehicle use and aims to provide immediate audible alerts when unsafe conditions are detected. Key constraints include real-time processing requirements, signal reliability from physiological sensors, and ensuring low computational latency while maintaining accuracy.

> [!abstract] Scope in one line
> Real-time detection of driver impairment via behavioural (eye closure, gaze, phone use) + physiological (heart rate) indicators, in-vehicle, with immediate audible alerts.

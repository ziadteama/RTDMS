# 1.1 Motivation

↑ [[01 Introduction]]

Road traffic accidents are a leading global safety challenge. Worldwide, about 1.3 million people die each year in crashes, costing roughly 3% of global GDP. Human factors — especially fatigue and distraction — drive a large share of these tragedies. For example, driver drowsiness is estimated to contribute to 3–30% of all crashes. In Egypt alone, 5,260 people were killed and 76,362 injured in road accidents in 2024, compared to 5,861 deaths and 71,016 injuries in 2023, according to the Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS). Earlier reports indicate that human factors account for approximately 64% of road accidents in Egypt, highlighting the dominant role of driver behaviour in crash occurrence. When tired, a driver's reaction time and attention deteriorate significantly; experiments show that heart rate drops by about 9% as a driver becomes sleepy.

Driver distraction is equally dangerous. Using a mobile phone or other non-driving task can take the eyes off the road for several seconds — enough to travel a significant distance without awareness, especially at highway speeds. In Egypt, most traffic accidents are attributed to driver-related causes such as speeding, inattention, and improper driving behaviour, reinforcing the critical impact of distraction on road safety. In practice, any diversion of visual attention (texting, talking on a phone, eating, etc.) or mental focus endangers safety.

Physiological monitoring, particularly heart rate (HR) and heart rate variability (HRV), adds an important internal layer to driver monitoring. Unlike camera-based cues that detect visible behaviour such as eye closure or head pose, heart rate signals can reveal changes in the driver's autonomic state at an earlier stage. Research shows that as alertness drops, autonomic balance shifts and HR/HRV patterns change, providing early warning signs of fatigue before obvious eyelid closure or lane deviation occurs. Heart rate monitoring also helps distinguish between different risk conditions, such as fatigue versus stress, allowing the system to respond more intelligently.

> - ~1.3M global road deaths/year, ~3% of global GDP
> - Drowsiness: 3–30% of crashes
> - Egypt 2024: 5,260 killed / 76,362 injured (vs. 5,861 / 71,016 in 2023) — [[4.1 Business Model|CAPMAS]]

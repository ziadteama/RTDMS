# 3.2 Technical Description

↑ [[03 Methodology]]

## Functional Requirements

These outline core system behaviours.

- Detect drowsiness via eye closure and head pose using camera on Raspberry Pi.
- Track gaze direction and flag deviations greater than two seconds off-road.
- Identify phone usage through hand and face analysis.
- Monitor heart rate and heart rate variability spikes on wearable, triggered by Raspberry Pi vision alerts.
- Trigger wearable vibration or buzz or both based on vision-detected risks, with escalating levels.

## Non-Functional Requirements

These ensure quality and usability.

- Real-time processing with latency less than 500 milliseconds end-to-end.
- Accuracy of at least 85% for vision detections across lighting conditions; heart rate and HRV sensitivity for spikes.
- Operate in automotive conditions from −10°C to 50°C with low power for wearable and Raspberry Pi.
- Reliable Raspberry Pi–wearable link such as Bluetooth with less than 100 millisecond trigger delay.
- Privacy-focused: local processing, no cloud or persistent data storage.

## Use-Case Scenarios

These scenarios cover primary operations and edge conditions to ensure robust performance.

- **Daylight Driving:** Clear visibility allows accurate eye closure and gaze tracking; system flags phone usage during traffic stops or highway cruising.
- **Driver with Glasses or Sunglasses:** Algorithms handle reflections and lens occlusions by combining multi-angle face analysis with heart rate variability confirmation.
- **Urban Distraction:** Rapid gaze shifts in city traffic trigger immediate alerts; phone proximity to face escalates to dual vibration and buzz.
- **Long Highway Drive:** Prolonged monitoring detects gradual drowsiness buildup, cross-verifying with hands on the steering wheel.

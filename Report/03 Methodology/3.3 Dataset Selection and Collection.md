# 3.3 Dataset Selection and Collection

↑ [[03 Methodology]]

The proposed DMS relies on carefully selected datasets to train and validate the AI models for drowsiness detection, gaze tracking, distraction detection, and physiological fatigue analysis. The dataset strategy combines established public datasets with a custom data collection plan designed to address the specific requirements of the proposed system and the Egyptian driving environment.

## 3.3.2 Dataset Requirements and Custom Data-Collection Plan

### 3.3.2.1 Normal Driving Baseline

Hands on the steering wheel, no phone usage. Drowsiness indicators include eye closure, slow blinking, head nodding, yawning, and drooping head posture. Distraction behaviours include looking away from the road, talking to passengers, and checking mirrors excessively. Phone usage scenarios include holding a phone, texting or browsing while driving, and looking down at the phone. Heart rate and HRV data are recorded during normal driving, drowsiness, distraction, and phone-usage scenarios.

### 3.3.2.2 Data Collection Plan

The custom dataset will be collected using a camera installed inside a vehicle when additional real-world samples are required. The Raspberry Pi Camera Module v3 or selected IR-capable camera module will be used at a resolution of 1920 × 1080 and a frame rate of 30 FPS. The camera will be mounted beside the right door of the vehicle, allowing the system to capture facial expressions, eye movement, head pose, and hand behaviour while reducing obstruction from the steering wheel and dashboard.

### 3.3.2.3 Sensor Integration

The data-collection setup integrates both vision and wearable physiological sensors. The vision sensor captures driver behaviour, while the wearable sensor records HR and HRV signals. When the vision system detects a possible risk, physiological data can be used as supporting evidence before escalating the alert level.

### 3.3.2.4 Participants and Consent

Approximately 10–20 volunteer drivers will participate in the custom data collection. Participants should represent different age groups, male and female drivers, and drivers wearing glasses or sunglasses. Each participant will sign a consent form allowing video and sensor recording for research purposes. All collected data will remain private and will only be used for system development and evaluation.

### 3.3.2.5 Data Annotation

After recording, the dataset will be labelled using annotation tools such as CVAT, LabelImg, or Roboflow. Labels will include driver behaviours such as eye closure, gaze direction, head pose, and phone usage.

### 3.3.2.6 Data Augmentation

To improve model robustness, data augmentation techniques can be applied to the visual dataset. These include image rotation, blur simulation, noise injection, and contrast variation. The purpose is to simulate additional lighting and cabin conditions without collecting a large amount of new data.

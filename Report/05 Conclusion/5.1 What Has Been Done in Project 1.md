# 5.1 What Has Been Done in Project 1

↑ [[05 Conclusion]]

During Project 1, the main foundation of the Real-Time Driver Monitoring System was completed. The project started by defining the motivation, problem statement, objectives, stakeholders, and user stories to clarify the need for a system that can detect driver drowsiness, distraction, gaze deviation, phone usage, and physiological fatigue indicators. A detailed literature survey was also conducted to study existing driver monitoring techniques, including vision-based drowsiness detection, gaze tracking, head pose estimation, phone-use detection, and physiological monitoring using heart rate and heart rate variability. Based on this research, the main research gaps were identified, especially the limitations of systems that depend on only one detection method. As a result, the proposed system was designed as a multimodal solution that combines behavioural monitoring through camera-based computer vision with physiological monitoring using wearable sensor data.

In addition, the system architecture and methodology were developed. The proposed architecture includes a Raspberry Pi processing unit, a camera module for monitoring eye closure, gaze direction, head pose, and phone usage, and a physiological monitoring subsystem for heart rate and HRV analysis. The decision-fusion stage was also defined to combine results from the vision-based models and physiological sensor readings before generating alerts. The alert subsystem was planned to provide buzzer and vibration warnings depending on the detected risk level. Dataset selection and collection planning were also completed by reviewing suitable public datasets for drowsiness, distraction, eye monitoring, gaze tracking, and physiological analysis, such as UTA-RLDD, AUC Distracted Driver Dataset, MRL Eye Dataset, DG-Unicamp Driver Gaze Dataset, and WESAD. A custom data collection plan was also proposed to support future testing under realistic driving conditions.

Project 1 also included the identification of the main software and hardware requirements needed for implementation. The selected hardware includes Raspberry Pi 5, Raspberry Pi AI HAT+, an IR-capable camera, the MAX30102 heart-rate sensor, and alerting components such as a buzzer and vibration motor. The software stack includes Python, OpenCV, MediaPipe Face Mesh, YOLO-based detection, sensor interface libraries, and annotation tools such as CVAT and Roboflow. Furthermore, business and feasibility studies were prepared, including SWOT analysis, PESTLE analysis, market benchmarking, risk analysis, and an initial cost calculation. Ethical considerations were also discussed, especially regarding privacy, local processing, biometric data, fairness, and driver comfort. Overall, Project 1 successfully completed the research, planning, system design, requirement analysis, dataset strategy, feasibility evaluation, and implementation roadmap required to prepare the system for development and testing in Project 2.

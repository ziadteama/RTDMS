# 5.2 Phase Two Plan

↑ [[05 Conclusion]]

The second phase of this project focuses on the transition from research and component selection to the delivery of a functional, real-time prototype. The plan is divided into five critical implementation phases.

## Phase 1: Hardware Assembly and Integration

The primary objective is to physically assemble the balanced real-time build identified during the market survey. Edge compute setup involves integrating the Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB) with the AI HAT+ (26 TOPS) to ensure sufficient computational headroom for multi-model inference. The vision system will use the Pi Camera Module mounted inside the vehicle cabin beside the right door to capture a full field of view including facial expressions and hand placement. The wearable prototype will finalize the custom-made wearable bracelet using the MAX30102 sensor and establish a stable Bluetooth communication link with a target trigger delay of less than 100 milliseconds.

## Phase 2: Model Training and Edge Optimization

This phase involves the deployment and fine-tuning of the selected AI frameworks on the Raspberry Pi. Behavioural models will deploy MediaPipe Face Mesh for high-speed tracking of the Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) and YOLOv5 for real-time mobile phone detection. Physiological classification will implement Random Forest or SVM algorithms to classify fatigue states based on Heart Rate (HR) and Heart Rate Variability (HRV) patterns gathered from the wearable. Latency optimization will ensure the system maintains an end-to-end latency of less than 500 milliseconds.

## Phase 3: Custom Data Collection and Annotation

To ensure the system is reliable in the Egyptian driving environment, a custom dataset will be developed. Participant testing will record data from 10–20 volunteer drivers representing diverse age groups, genders, and scenarios, including drivers wearing glasses or sunglasses. Annotation tools such as CVAT or Roboflow will label custom footage for drowsiness (PERCLOS), distraction (gaze deviation), and unsafe behaviours. Data augmentation techniques including image rotation, blur simulation, and noise injection will improve model robustness across varying lighting and cabin conditions.

## Phase 4: Sensor Fusion and Alert Logic Implementation

The core multimodal logic will be programmed to handle risk verification and intervention. Multi-modal correlation will use physiological markers (HR/HRV) to cross-verify vision-based cues, ensuring that alerts for drowsiness are confirmed by biological shifts. The alert system will be coded to trigger voice recordings and buzzers within one second of risk detection. An emergency escalation protocol will ensure that prolonged unresponsiveness or critical impairment results in the system contacting the nearest hospital based on GPS location.

> [!important] New in Phase 2 vs. Project 1 scope
> The **GPS-based emergency-escalation protocol** (contacting the nearest hospital on prolonged unresponsiveness) is introduced here — it is not part of the original Project 1 functional/non-functional requirements in [[3.2 Technical Description]]. Track it as a new requirement + privacy implication (GPS is location data, relevant to [[4.3 Ethics]]).

## Phase 5: Validation and Final Prototype Testing

The project concludes with rigorous testing against the SMART objectives defined in Project 1. Accuracy benchmarking will validate that the system achieves at least 85–90% detection accuracy for eye closure, gaze deviation, and phone usage. Environmental stress testing will ensure stable performance across day and night conditions and reliable operation in vehicle temperatures ranging from −10°C to 50°C. Final prototype delivery will complete the integration into a self-contained, privacy-focused offline unit that processes all data locally without cloud dependency.
